## The problem

It started with a business owner who asked me for help with marketing design. As we talked, it was clear his problem was bigger than design. He struggled to create content consistently, to understand his audience, and to run marketing without expensive help.

So I spoke to more small business owners, growing D2C brands and marketing teams. The same pattern kept coming up:

> The hard part isn't making content. It's knowing what to make, why it matters and how to make it work.

Most of them relied on a mix of disconnected tools, freelancers or agencies. The AI tools they tried generated individual assets, but none of them understood the brand or its market.

## Who I designed for

Small business owners, growing D2C brands and their marketing teams. They need a steady stream of posts, ads and videos but can't afford an agency, so they patch it together with separate tools and freelancers.

## Research

My research was conversations, then building and listening. I talked with business owners, D2C brands and marketing teams to understand how they decide what to post. Then I built an early prototype, onboarded beta users and refined the product from their feedback.

What I learned shaped the core idea: connect **brand intelligence**, **marketing strategy** and **creative production** in one place, and make the marketing capabilities of a large company available to growing brands.

## Key decisions

<Shift n="01" from="An AI creative generator" to="A marketing platform, from research to production">
  I started with AI-generated creatives. The more I explored the problem, the clearer it became that making content
  wasn't the only struggle. Businesses also struggled to understand their audience, research competitors and decide
  what to say. So I built a complete workflow, from research and strategy to creative production.
</Shift>

<Shift n="02" from="Prompt in, generic content out" to="Research first, then content">
  I didn't want SkalX to produce generic AI content. I added brand and competitor intelligence so every creative is
  informed by the business, its positioning and its market, not just a text prompt. That's why each ad carries the
  reason behind it.
</Shift>

<Shift n="03" from="More integrations, more features" to="A focused core and simpler pricing">
  More integrations and features wouldn't necessarily make the product more valuable. I paused some publishing and
  advertising integrations and put that effort into the core: research, strategy and content creation. I also moved
  to a subscription and credit-based pricing model.
</Shift>

## Outcome

SkalX is live at [skalxai.app](https://skalxai.app/). During the early beta it brought in its first paying customer and about ₹8,000–₹10,000 in revenue. In September 2026 I was selected for the iTNT iAccelerate Go-to-Market and Investment Readiness Cohort, where I'm working on the business side: go-to-market, customer acquisition, business model and investment readiness.

## What I'd do differently

I'd validate the commercial opportunity before investing so heavily in building. I spent a lot of time designing, developing and integrating features because I wanted a complete product. Looking back, I should have put a smaller version in front of paying customers much earlier, and started sales conversations alongside development instead of after it.

> Building a product people can use and building a business people will pay for are two very different challenges. SkalX taught me both.
