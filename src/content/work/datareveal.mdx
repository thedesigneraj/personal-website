## The problem

Walmart's CFO team needed a faster way to find, understand and work with financial information. Their workflows meant moving between financial data, reports and different sources, so it was hard to get one consolidated view, and a lot of time went into repetitive tasks.

The goal was to explore how AI-powered automation could simplify these workflows, make financial insights easier to reach and make analysis more efficient.

## My role

DataReveal was a startup, so I worked close to the decisions. I collaborated with the Product Manager and a team of five or more developers, and talked directly with DataReveal's CEO and Walmart's CFO team, who were both the main stakeholders and the people who'd use the product.

My job was to connect three things: what the business needed, what the users needed, and what the developers could realistically build.

## Research

Before designing anything, I wanted to understand how the team actually worked and where the friction was.

## Key decisions

### One workspace instead of many screens

The main dashboard became a central workspace for the CFO team, bringing data visualisations, reports and analytics into one interface. People could get an overview of what mattered without jumping between screens.

### A clear path from overview to detail

I set up an information hierarchy that let people start at the high-level numbers and move into detailed financial reports when they needed to. Every choice aimed to reduce the mental effort of working with complex financial data.

### A design system the team could share

Instead of designing elements again and again, I built a reusable design system: components with consistent styles, behaviours and variants, plus the layouts, typography and visual styles the product needed. It gave the product one visual language and gave developers a shared reference when turning designs into code. It also made the design easier to maintain as the product grew.

## Outcome

- Designed an enterprise product around the real workflows of Walmart's CFO team.
- Worked across business, product and engineering, turning complex stakeholder needs into usable product experiences.
- Built a reusable design system that kept the product consistent and gave design and development a common foundation.

## What I'd do differently

I'd bring in a more structured validation process from the start: document the existing workflows in more detail, set measurable UX success criteria, and run usability tests with end users at different stages.

I'd also set baseline metrics early, like task completion time, workflow efficiency and user satisfaction, so the product's impact could be measured instead of assumed.

> Good product design goes beyond an intuitive interface. It means understanding the business, listening to users, working closely with engineering, and balancing all three.
